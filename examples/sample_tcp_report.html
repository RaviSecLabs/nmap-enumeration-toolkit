Executive summary

The illustrative assessment identifies potentially exposed services, weak legacy protocols and opportunities for unauthorised information access. Priority should be given to validating anonymous FTP access and exposed SMB shares, followed by reviewing Telnet, legacy SMB settings and NFS exports. No successful exploitation is claimed by this report.

Overall sample posture: Elevated exposure in a deliberately vulnerable lab environment. Findings require verification before they are described as confirmed vulnerabilities.

Priority findings and potential impact

HIGH · Lead

FTP may allow anonymous login · TCP 21

Potential impact: An unauthenticated visitor may read files and, if write access is enabled, modify or upload content.

Example evidence to look for: ftp-anon: Anonymous FTP login allowed · Script: ftp-anon · Action: verify accessible directories and permissions; disable anonymous access if unnecessary.
HIGH · Lead

SMB shares may expose data · TCP 445

Potential impact: Guest-readable or writable shares may expose sensitive files or allow unauthorised modification.

Evidence to verify: share list and access rights · Scripts: smb-enum-shares, smb-security-mode · Action: restrict guest access and apply least privilege.
MEDIUM · Lead

Telnet exposed · TCP 23

Potential impact: Telnet commonly transmits credentials and commands without encryption, exposing sessions to interception on accessible networks.

Evidence: service detection shows Telnet · Script: telnet-encryption · Action: replace with SSH and restrict access.
MEDIUM · Lead

Legacy SMB protocol support · TCP 139/445

Potential impact: Older SMB versions can increase exposure to protocol weaknesses and downgrade risks.

Evidence to verify: smb-protocols results · Action: disable SMBv1 where possible and update Samba.
MEDIUM · Lead

NFS exports may be accessible · TCP 2049

Potential impact: Broad export permissions can disclose files to unintended clients.

Evidence to verify: exported paths and allowed clients · Script: nfs-showmount · Action: restrict export networks and permissions.

Evidence and validation approach

  • Confirmed: Supported by direct output or a separate authorised verification step.
  • Suspected: A service, configuration or version suggests a possible weakness, but impact is not yet demonstrated.
  • Informational: Relevant inventory or configuration data without a demonstrated weakness.

For a real report, preserve the exact Nmap command, script names, timestamp, relevant output lines and source filename for every finding. Record false positives and validation outcomes.

Recommended remediation priorities

  1. Remove anonymous or guest access where not required; verify file permissions.
  2. Replace Telnet with SSH and restrict administrative access.
  3. Disable obsolete SMB versions and review Samba configuration.
  4. Restrict NFS exports to explicitly authorised hosts.
  5. Update exposed services and rerun the same enumeration checks.
